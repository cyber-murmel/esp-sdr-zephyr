The MCH2022 badge carries an ESP32, an iCE40UP5K FPGA and an RP2040. This page is the design of
what runs on the two chips beside the ESP32, the RP2040's firmware and the FPGA's gateware, and of
the links that carry the ESP32's samples to the PC. It follows
[mch2022_coproc](../../apps/mch2022_coproc/README.rst) in [Applications](index.md).

What each chip does on the board, the RP2040's flash layout and the ways in and out of its
bootloader are on the board page,
[MCH2022 badge (RP2040 coprocessor)](../../boards/badgeteam/mch2022_rp2040/doc/index.rst).

Usage is in the READMEs of [mch2022_coproc](../../apps/mch2022_coproc/README.rst),
[osmosdr_serial](../../apps/osmosdr_serial/README.rst) and
[micropython](../../apps/micropython/README.rst).

## The RP2040 coprocessor

Design note, 2026-11-03/04. [mch2022_coproc](../../apps/mch2022_coproc/README.rst) replaces the
stock firmware of the badge's RP2040 (`boards/badgeteam/mch2022_rp2040`), so that
[osmosdr_serial](../../apps/osmosdr_serial/README.rst) is no longer limited by the link. The app's
README says how to build and install it;
[MCH2022 badge (RP2040 coprocessor)](../../boards/badgeteam/mch2022_rp2040/doc/index.rst) has the
ways back to the stock firmware.

### Why replace the stock firmware

- The ESP32 reaches USB through the RP2040's UART bridge.
- The stock firmware polls both UARTs from one main loop, without DMA, with their 32-byte FIFOs
  and 256-byte USB buffers.
- 2 Mbaud worked, at 200 kB/s; 3 Mbaud got no answer.
- At 2 MS/s the ESP32's library makes some 676 kS/s on average (2044 samples a 3.025 ms burst,
  [Burst timing on the ESP32](#burst-timing-on-the-esp32)), 1.35 MB/s even as 8-bit pairs.

### Compatibility with the stock firmware

Everything that talks to the stock firmware goes on working, whatever it runs on. The interfaces
kept are listed in [mch2022_coproc](../../apps/mch2022_coproc/README.rst); this section has their
details.

#### USB descriptors

- The strings "Badge.team" and "MCH2022 badge", and the flash's unique ID as serial number, so the
  `/dev/serial/by-id` names stay.
- The configuration descriptor is the stock one byte for byte: CDC ACM on interfaces 0 to 3 with
  its endpoints, WebUSB on 4 and 5.
- It differs in the new interface 6, the string indexes that interface shifts, and 64 instead of
  32 bytes on the WebUSB endpoints.
- The binary object store is the same.
- The Microsoft OS 2.0 set has a third function, the first two unchanged.

#### WebUSB requests

The firmware answers the WebUSB class requests 0x22 to 0x27 and stalls the rest.

#### Reset by DTR and RTS

- The stock sequences reset the ESP32, in application or download mode, with the reset lock.
- `west flash` connects in 3.6 s (20 of 20) and writes as fast as with the stock firmware.
- `COPROC_RESET_CIRCUIT` is the usual two-transistor circuit instead: esptool's first sequence
  works and it connects in 1.4 s (20 of 20).

#### I2C registers

- The I2C coprocessor keeps all its registers.
- Register 0 still reads 0x0C: the launcher replaces anything below that and reads MSC registers
  above it.
- Registers 100 to 103 read 0x5A, the API's version and feature bits.

#### Stock bugs not repeated

- IO0 stayed low after a download reset until the next normal one (it is also the codec's clock).
- `GPIO_IN` always read 0.
- The backlight's register read 0 until written.
- A WS2812 trigger with the mode off hung with more than four LEDs.

## The RP2040 firmware

Zephyr on one core, from the flash behind the badge's bootloader. What it adds to the stock
firmware (`include/esp_sdr/mch2022_coproc.h`):

- Both UARTs on DMA, so nothing is lost at the ESP32's 5 Mbaud. The stock firmware polls them, and
  [osmosdr_serial](../../apps/osmosdr_serial/README.rst) stopped at 2 Mbaud
  ([UARTs on DMA](#uarts-on-dma)).
- Interface 6, a bulk pipe to the ESP32's UART with requests for the chip itself: counters, log,
  resets, the ROM's USB bootloader ([USB bulk engine](#usb-bulk-engine)).
- The FPGA's fast line into that pipe, 15 Mbaud from the FPGA with the link bitstream
  ([The fast line](#the-fast-line)).
- Registers 100 to 103, by which the ESP32 can tell it from the stock firmware
  ([I2C registers](#i2c-registers)).

### UARTs on DMA

- Zephyr's PL011 driver sets up the line; `uart_dma.c` owns DMACR and DR through the hal's DMA
  calls.
- Zephyr's DMA driver has no ring mode, and its devicetree node stays off so that it does not take
  channels.
- Receive runs for good into a ring: 16 KiB for the ESP32, 4 KiB for the FPGA. The channel's
  remaining count says how far it got.
- Transmit takes what lies together in a 4 KiB ring; its end interrupt starts the next piece.

### USB bulk engine

`usb_vendor.c` and `usb_dev.c` make every function one class on one bulk engine. A thread
(`bridge.c`) moves the data, woken by completions, by the UARTs' DMA and every millisecond for what
the UARTs bring. What follows was learnt on the badge.

#### CDC buffers

- Zephyr's CDC ACM class sends one packet per buffer and goes through a thread for each.
- That misses the frame nearly every time: 69 kB/s where the UART brings 200.
- The CDC ports here send buffers of 1 KiB, re-armed per packet in the interrupt.

#### OUT transfers

- The controller ends an OUT transfer at a short packet or a full buffer.
- Linux's cdc-acm and the WebUSB tools send no zero-length packet after a write of whole packets.
  With larger buffers the end of such a write waited for more to come.
- So their OUT buffers are one packet each: with a thread between packets, 150 to 190 kB/s, enough
  for esptool's 92.
- Interface 6's host ends every write with a short packet and gets 1 KiB buffers: 448 kB/s at
  5 Mbaud.

#### IN double buffering

An IN endpoint with one buffer is empty when the host's next token comes before the interrupt has
refilled it. The host gets a NAK and comes back much later.

| IN endpoint | Throughput |
| --- | --- |
| One buffer | 520 to 660 kB/s |
| Two buffers, an interrupt per pair (TinyUSB's way: empty after each pair) | 553 kB/s |
| Two buffers, an interrupt for each (`CONFIG_UDC_RPI_PICO_DOUBLE_BUFFER_BULK_IN`, in our Zephyr) | 928 kB/s |

#### The bridge thread

- The thread moves what is there when it looks and then looks again, rather than until a ring is
  empty.
- Bytes can come as fast as it takes them, in buffers of a few bytes: at 2 Mbaud it once stayed in
  that loop for a whole 10 s transfer, and the [watchdog](#watchdog) rebooted the badge.
- A closed CDC port is not told apart from a slow one on USB: what nobody reads for 200 ms is
  dropped, so the next reader gets no old news.

### I2C registers and the FPGA reset

`regmap.c` and `i2c_regs.c` serve the I2C coprocessor:

- Bytes come and go in the driver's interrupt.
- The effects of writes wait in a work item until the stop, in ascending register order, as in the
  stock firmware.
- CRESET moves at the stop itself.
- CDONE is read when the ESP32 asks, which it does right after the bitstream's last byte.

#### The DesignWare race

- The interrupt can be 100 us late, with code running from flash; where that comes from was not
  found.
- That is three bytes at 400 kHz, and the ESP32's load writes, reads and writes with little time
  between.
- The DesignWare driver then took one transaction's pointer for the last one's data, in 5 to 15 %
  of the boots, which left the FPGA unloaded.
- Our Zephyr tells transactions apart by the flag on a first byte.

### SELECT, boot and watchdog

#### SELECT

SELECT (`qspi_select.c`) is the flash's chip select. It can only be read from RAM with every
interrupt off and the pin let go, some 30 us every 30 ms. DMA goes on meanwhile.

#### Boot

- The badge's bootloader starts the image; its start conditions are on
  [MCH2022 badge (RP2040 coprocessor)](../../boards/badgeteam/mch2022_rp2040/doc/index.rst).
- The watchdog's scratch registers are read and cleared first thing: the bootloader looks at them,
  and a crash leaves its record there (register 18).
- The ESP32 stays in reset until the I2C target runs.

#### Watchdog

- 2 s, fed by the bridge thread, the lowest of the firmware's threads.
- Anything that hangs reboots the badge, the ESP32 with it, rather than need its power cut.
- A DMA abort that waited for ever did exactly that on the desk before the watchdog was there.
- It is off for the ROM's bootloader.

## The FPGA accelerator

The badge carries an iCE40UP5K next to its ESP32. The gateware in `gateware/` (Amaranth) makes it
the badge's display controller and spectrum engine:

- The ESP32 sends I/Q samples and its user interface over one SPI port.
- The FPGA computes the spectrum and draws a spectrum trace and a waterfall straight to the
  ILI9341, at the panel's rate.

### What runs where

| | ESP32 | FPGA |
|---|---|---|
| Panel | Zephyr's ILI9341 driver and LVGL, through `esdr,mch2022-fpga-dbi` | 8080 bus, passthrough, its own regions |
| Samples | bursts from `lib/esp_sdr`, sent as they are (WORD32) or packed | 8192-sample ring |
| Spectrum | settings in dB (`esdr_fpga_dsp_config()`) | window, FFT 16..512, power, log2, average, columns |
| Graphics | palette, region placement | waterfall ring (64 KiB), trace, rendering per TE |

#### The panel's bus

- The FPGA owns the panel's bus while the ESP32 holds LCD_MODE (IO26) high.
- The ESP32 then floats its D/C and CS, which meet the FPGA's WR and CS through 47 ohm, and keeps
  the panel's reset.
- Without a bitstream the panel controller drives the panel over SPI as before.

### The SPI link

One SPI frame (CS low) is one command. The opcodes, memory windows and registers are in
`include/esp_sdr/esdr_fpga_regs.h`, generated from `gateware/esdr_fpga/regs.py`.

| Opcode | Payload |
|---|---|
| 0x01 WRITE | address, 16-bit words |
| 0x02 READ | address, count, two pad bytes; then the words come back |
| 0x03 SAMPLES | format (WORD32, IQ10P, IQ8, IQ16; RESYNC), samples |
| 0x04 DBI | flags (HOLD, NOCMD), command, data: the panel's traffic |
| 0x05 WFLINE | one waterfall line of palette indices |

#### Clock rates

- Writes run at 40 MHz (about 5 MB/s), reads at 20 MHz.
- MISO comes through the GPIO matrix, and at 26.7 MHz and above reads fail.
- Reads must be read-only SPI chunks: the classic ESP32's DMA cannot do a write phase and a read
  phase in one half-duplex transaction (Zephyr commit "drivers: spi: esp32: no write phase before
  half-duplex reads").

### The DSP chain

Per frame:

1. The window (Q15, from `esp_sdr_window()`) and a halving.
2. A radix-2 FFT that halves every stage.
3. `L = 256 log2 |X|^2` plus 512 per halving plus an offset that puts a full-scale tone at 0 dB.
4. The average `s += F(L - s)`, from a table that makes it the linear exponential average of
   `esdr.psd()`.
5. Every few frames, the columns (the max of their bins, with a start bin and step for zoom),
   scaled into a waterfall line and the trace's rows.

`model.py` is the reference, bit for bit.

#### Throughput on the badge

Measured on the badge (N = 512, 30 MHz):

| | |
|---|---|
| frames per second | about 1860 (950 kS/s) |
| a full-scale tone at +fs/8 | column 200 of 320, 0.1 dB |
| radio bursts of 16380 at 40 MS/s, WORD32 | 73 per second: capture 0.43 ms, send 13.3 ms |
| the same, IQ10P / IQ8 | 102 / 127 per second (packing 1.1 / 0.8 ms) |

- With interrupt-driven SPI (`CONFIG_SPI_ESP32_INTERRUPT`, which the driver implies) the CPU is
  free while a frame goes out.
- The FFT, not the link, limits the throughput.

### The renderer

- Two regions, a waterfall and a spectrum trace, each placed in screen pixels.
- Every pixel is a palette index: 248 colours for the waterfall, the rest for the trace and centre
  line.
- The renderer writes with the host's MADCTL minus MV, so a write runs along a native panel row.
- It does one memory write per row, so the host gets the bus between any two rows when it has
  bytes queued or holds the bus (DBI flag HOLD, set by the panel controller from CASET/PASET
  against the bytes written).
- A full 320x240 screen takes about 10.5 ms; on the TE edge (88 per second on this panel) that is
  44 renders per second.

### Sample formats

`gateware/tools/bfp_eval.py` compares formats on real bursts (`fpga dump`): eight bursts at 16, 40
and 80 MS/s, spectra at N = 512 against the lossless 10 bits, worst case:

| Format | Bits per pair | Floor shift | Worst error, top 40 dB | Weak tone's margin lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IQ10P | 20 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| BFP8 (16 pairs share an exponent) | 16.5 | 0.28 dB | 0.41 dB | 0.1 dB |
| IQ8 | 16 | 0.73 dB | 1.20 dB | 0.1 dB |
| BFP6 | 12.5 | 3.0 dB | 3.7 dB | 1.5 dB |
| BFP4 | 8.5 | 13.1 dB | 10.8 dB | 8.8 dB |
| NVFP4 (E2M1, E4M3 scale per 16) | 9 | 13.1 dB | 10.7 dB | 8.7 dB |
| 4-bit | 8 | 17.0 dB | 16.7 dB | 7.5 dB |

Only BFP8 stays within 0.5 dB of the 10 bits, for 17 % fewer bytes. With the FFT as the limit
([Throughput on the badge](#throughput-on-the-badge)) that buys nothing, so the gateware has IQ10P
and IQ8 and no block formats.

### Fitting the UP5K

- The gateware fills 4982 of 5280 logic cells (all 4 SPRAMs, 18 of 30 block RAMs, 3 of 8
  multipliers) at 32 MHz.
- To fit, the FFT stops at 512 points (the screen has 320 columns), and it halves every stage
  instead of keeping a block exponent.
- The trace has no fill or grid.
- The test source (a tone generator) is gone: the ESP32 makes test samples.
- The UART probe line is a build variant (`debug`).
- New features need cells found first: `synth_ice40 -noflatten` with yosys `stat` shows them per
  module.

#### The LED

- The LED fades on the UP5K's hard PWM (`SB_LEDDA_IP`), which the driver sets up over one register
  at boot.
- Colour and brightness come from `CONFIG_ESDR_FPGA_LED_RED`, `_GREEN` and `_BLUE`.
- They stay out of the bitstream on purpose: this close to full, new constants alone moved the
  clock from 32.1 to 28.75 MHz, which fails, and the seed moves it as far (29.2 to 32.25 MHz).

### Building and loading

```sh
nix-shell shell.nix
cd gateware
python3 -m esdr_fpga.build top --out <dir>     # bitstream <dir>/top/top.bin, utilisation, fmax
python3 -m pytest tests                       # simulations against model.py
python3 -m esdr_fpga.regs > ../include/esp_sdr/esdr_fpga_regs.h
```

#### Loading the bitstream

- `CONFIG_ESDR_FPGA_IMAGE` embeds a bitstream; the driver loads it at boot (38 ms).
- Zephyr's `lattice,ice40-fpga` driver configures the FPGA at 25 MHz, the configuration port's
  limit in the data sheet; frames run at 40 MHz.
- The image goes over SPI3 with SS low.
- The board holds the FPGA in reset until a load (`creset-hold`).
- CRESET (the RP2040's register 5, bit 0) and CDONE go through a GPIO driver on the RP2040. When
  CRESET moves and CDONE is read is in
  [I2C registers and the FPGA reset](#i2c-registers-and-the-fpga-reset).

## Links to the host

[osmosdr_serial](../../apps/osmosdr_serial/README.rst)'s blocks reach the RP2040's interface 6 in
two ways: framed on the ESP32's UART, or through the FPGA on the fast line. The port strings and
options of the host tools are in the app's README; [Host transports](../samples/transports.md)
compares the serial links of all boards.

### The framed link

`apps/osmosdr_serial`'s plain transport on the UART sends blocks of 4096 bytes whatever they hold,
has no checksum and resynchronises on a magic number. On the badge's UART, without flow control
wires, `esp_sdr/esdr_link.h` replaces it. A frame says what it is and how many came before it, and
a lost byte costs that frame, not the stream.

#### Frames

- Type, flags, sequence number, payload, CRC-32, the whole COBS encoded and ended by a zero.
- Requests and replies carry `esdr_proto.h`'s frames as they are, so the handlers did not change.
- A block goes by its length.

#### Who speaks what

- The firmware listens for both at all times.
- A host that says hello gets frames. A plain host opens with thousands of zeros, and 64 empty
  frames in a row go back to the plain transport.
- Nothing needs to be told or configured, and a plain host still works on either RP2040 firmware.
- The plan had the ESP32 ask the RP2040's registers. This way also works on any bridge, and follows
  the host rather than the firmware.

#### Credit

- The host grants wire bytes (16 KiB at hello, more as it reads), and the ESP32 sends a block only
  with credit for it.
- So a host that stops reading stops the sender, instead of the RP2040's 16 KiB ring overrunning.
- A new burst simply waits: its gap to the last is flagged, as ever.

#### Rate

- `ESDR_REQ_SET_LINK_BAUD` changes the rate, in the old rate's reply; both ends move and the host
  says hello again.
- A host that finds no answer at 2 Mbaud (its predecessor went away at 5) resets the ESP32 over USB
  and starts anew.
- On closing, a host goes back to 2 Mbaud for whoever comes next.

#### The hosts

- `FramedLink` in `apps/osmosdr/soapy/SoapyEsdr.cpp` finds the badge by itself and gives its callers
  the padded blocks they expect.
- `EsdrFramed` in `apps/osmosdr_serial/tools/esdr_serial.py` is the other.
- Both run over libusb on interface 6.

#### UART or FPGA

- With the FPGA's link bitstream loaded, as osmosdr_serial's badge image has it, blocks go through
  the FPGA ([The fast line](#the-fast-line)); else at up to 5 Mbaud on the UART.
- On the UART the limit is the ESP32's time. A burst takes 2 to 3 ms to capture and decimate, in
  which the UART's FIFO runs dry, and its 4 KiB take 8 ms at 5 Mbaud.
- That is some 80 bursts a second of the 330 (2 MS/s) to 525 (125 kS/s) the library could make,
  so blocks also have a way that needs no CPU.

### The fast line

Blocks go from the ESP32 by SPI DMA into the FPGA, from there on a UART of its own to the RP2040,
which merges them onto interface 6 with what the ESP32's UART brings. The requests, replies and
text stay on that UART; the host grants credit and asks for the fast line with the same few words.

```
ESP32 --SPI3 DMA, 40 MHz--> FPGA 32 KiB FIFO --15 Mbaud--> RP2040 PIO --DMA--> 32 KiB ring --+
  |                                   ^                       |                              +--> USB
  +----- UART0 (requests, replies)    +--- ready (GPIO24) ----+  UART0 16 KiB ring ----------+
```

#### FPGA side

- `gateware/esdr_fpga/link.py` is a bitstream of its own (`link.bin`): 599 of 5280 cells, 44.87 MHz
  against the 30 asked for.
- SPI frames with opcode 0x20 (`ESDR_LINK_FPGA_OP`) go into a FIFO in one SPRAM block.
- They go out at 30 MHz / 2 = 15 Mbaud, 8N1, on pin 9.
- A low ready line on pin 6 holds it back at the next frame's end.
- Bit 1 of register CAPS says the bitstream has all this; registers count what is free, sent, lost
  and held.

#### RP2040 side

- While the line is on (`COPROC_REQ_FPGA_FASTLINK`, `uart_dma.c`), the FPGA's two UART pins change
  hands.
- GPIO25 goes to a PIO state machine at 8 times the bit rate and through DMA into a ring.
- GPIO24 is the ready line: low above 24 KiB in the ring, high again below 12 KiB.
- `bridge.c` merges the two rings at zero bytes, so that frames stay whole. A frame that does not go
  on (a boot message has no zero) gives way after 10 ms.

#### ESP32 side

- In `apps/osmosdr_serial` (`CONFIG_APP_LINK_FAST`) the host's hello says it wants the line.
- The device says yes if the FPGA is loaded and has the capability. Else it stays on the UART, and
  so does a host that does not ask.
- Blocks and other frames have sequence numbers of their own, for they may overtake each other.
- The credit (32 KiB) bounds what is under way, so the FIFO cannot overflow.
- An FPGA with another bitstream on it answers no to the capability and leaves the pins to their
  UART.

## Measurements

### Link throughput

On the badge, the ESP32 sending and checking a counter on its UART through the firmware to the
host, 5 s per case (`uart_overrun` and `ring_overrun` 0 throughout; nothing lost in any case):

| rate | ESP32 to host, CDC | host to ESP32, CDC | ESP32 to host, interface 6 | host to ESP32, interface 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Mbaud | 200 kB/s | 151 to 187 kB/s | 200 kB/s | 201 kB/s |
| 4 Mbaud | 400 kB/s | 152 to 188 kB/s | 400 kB/s | 400 kB/s |
| 5 Mbaud | 500 kB/s | 152 kB/s | 499 kB/s | 448 kB/s |

- A longer run, 60 s each way at 2 and 5 Mbaud on both routes, 168 MB in all, lost nothing either.
- 5 Mbaud is the ESP32's limit.
- USB alone (`COPROC_TEST_SOURCE`, a counter from the firmware to a host reading 64 KiB at a time)
  gives 928 kB/s, no word lost.

### Stream rates

[osmosdr_serial](../../apps/osmosdr_serial/README.rst) by its average rate of samples, in 4 to 14 s
at the desk (all blocks whole):

| Stream | Plain, 2 Mbaud | Framed, 2 Mbaud | Framed, 5 Mbaud | Framed, FPGA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 MS/s | 46.4 kS/s | 82.2 kS/s | 157.8 kS/s | 449.6 kS/s |
| 1 MS/s | 44.8 kS/s | 77.7 kS/s | 139.7 kS/s | 415.8 kS/s |
| 500 kS/s | 22.8 kS/s | 70.4 kS/s | 113.6 kS/s | 236.0 kS/s |
| 250 kS/s | 11.5 kS/s | 59.1 kS/s | 82.6 kS/s | 126.1 kS/s |
| 125 kS/s | not measured (a single failed start, see [A failed start](#a-failed-start)) | 44.7 kS/s | 53.0 kS/s | 64.8 kS/s |

- The FPGA's column is through SoapySDR.
- The other columns were measured before the burst cycle got shorter
  ([Burst timing on the ESP32](#burst-timing-on-the-esp32)). At 2 MS/s they now reach 89.1 kS/s
  plain and 178.9 kS/s framed at 5 Mbaud.

#### Through the FPGA

- At 2 MS/s through the FPGA, 450 kS/s, some 900 kB/s, which is USB's limit: the ESP32 could give
  670 kS/s.
- A minute through SoapySDR: 443 kS/s, none dropped.
- A minute with every frame checked: no bad or lost frame, no framing error, the ready line never
  low.
- Another figure for the fast line: with the link bitstream, about 700 kB/s of osmosdr_serial's
  blocks, where the UART takes 300. It disagrees with the 450 kS/s, some 900 kB/s, measured above.

### Burst timing on the ESP32

Below 2 MS/s the ESP32 sets the pace. What a burst takes (16380 samples at 16 MS/s, in
microseconds):

| | 2 MS/s | 1 MS/s | 500 kS/s | 125 kS/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| capture (the engine, no CPU) | 1022 | 1024 | 1023 | 1017 |
| CIC | 1720 | 1270 | 1043 | 869 |
| samples into a block | 283 | 142 | 72 | 19 |
| COBS and CRC, while the capture runs | 627 | 318 | 161 | 42 |
| SPI, by DMA, while the rest runs | 871 | 455 | 250 | 95 |

- So a burst is capture, CIC and packing in a row, as long as encoding fits inside the capture.
- The CIC costs 12 cycles an input and 105 an output of its 240 MHz. The outputs' share is register
  pressure: it has six integrators and six comb states.
- The CIC is the next thing to go after, or the second core.

#### Burst time cuts

It was 5.9 ms a burst at 2 MS/s before these changes, each measured with cycle counters on the
badge:

- The ESP32's SPI driver spun through every transfer: its interrupt mode set the interrupt flag
  itself and did the whole transfer inside the handler, 860 us with the CPU stopped. It now starts
  a transfer and goes on at the hardware's interrupt.
- A 64-bit multiply per CIC output went to libgcc (now `mulsh`, the result the same for every
  factor).
- A function call per sample into the block: 117 cycles a sample, now 33.
- CRC-32 by the nibble (now the ROM's, by the byte) and COBS through memory (now in registers).
- A frame encoded anew for every look at the credit (twice a burst at the top rate).
- A burst's last 4 samples as a frame of their own (bursts are whole blocks now).
- SoapySDR reading one transfer at a time, which left USB idle for a tenth of its time (now 4
  queued, and decoding in a thread of its own).

#### The SPI write beside the capture

- The SPI write now runs while the next capture does, both by DMA into or out of internal RAM.
- A tone through the chain (the WHY badge's C6, 900 kHz off) would show a lost word as a phase step
  of 0.35 rad inside a burst. It shows the same as with the write spinning, no overlap.
- The largest step in a burst is 0.45 rad median, 0.54 at most in 5000 bursts (its transmitter's
  own sidebands). With one word's step added in software, the same bursts show 0.56 and up in 95 %
  of them.

## Checks and open points

### A failed start

- One start of the plain transport failed once with `SET_FREQ: Invalid argument`, right after a
  250 kS/s run.
- 46 later starts did not fail, 16 of them in that same order.
- 30 before and 30 after the idle sleep went from 1 ms (200 bytes at 2 Mbaud, against
  the ESP32's FIFO of 128) to 100 us, so that change is reasoned, not proven.

### Checked by hand

- Every button with SELECT and the joystick as the ESP32 reads them (twice).
- The launcher by joystick after a cold start.
- The backlight's steps.
- The IR LED in a phone's camera.

### Checked by test

- `integration.esdr_fpga` passes (4 of 4) through this firmware.
- The FPGA's bitstream loaded at 240 of 240 resets of the ESP32 under that test's image.
