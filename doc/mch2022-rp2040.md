# The MCH2022 badge's RP2040

Design note, 2026-11-03/04. `apps/mch2022_coproc` replaces the stock firmware of the badge's RP2040
(`boards/badgeteam/mch2022_rp2040`), so that `apps/osmosdr_serial` is no longer limited by the
link. The app's README says how to build and install it; the board's documentation has the ways
back to the stock firmware.

## Why

The ESP32 reaches USB through the RP2040's UART bridge. The stock firmware polls both UARTs from
one main loop, no DMA, with their 32-byte FIFOs and 256-byte USB buffers. 2 Mbaud worked, 200 kB/s;
3 Mbaud got no answer. At 2 MS/s the ESP32's library makes some 2 MB/s of samples.

## Compatibility

Everything that talks to the stock firmware goes on working, whatever it runs on:

- USB: 16d0:0f9a, "Badge.team", "MCH2022 badge", the flash's unique ID as serial number, so the
  `/dev/serial/by-id` names stay. The configuration descriptor is the stock one byte for byte
  (CDC ACM on interfaces 0 to 3 with its endpoints, WebUSB on 4 and 5) but for the new interface 6, the
  string indexes it shifts and 64 instead of 32 bytes on the WebUSB endpoints; the binary object
  store is the same, the Microsoft OS 2.0 set has a third function with the first two unchanged.
- The WebUSB class requests 0x22 to 0x27, and stalling the rest.
- DTR and RTS: the stock sequences reset the ESP32, in application or download mode, with the reset
  lock. `west flash` connects in 3.6 s (20 of 20) and writes as fast as with the stock firmware.
  `COPROC_RESET_CIRCUIT` is the usual two-transistor circuit instead: esptool's first sequence
  works and it connects in 1.4 s (20 of 20).
- I2C at 0x17: all registers, register 0 still 0x0C (the launcher replaces anything below that and
  reads MSC registers above it). Registers 100 to 103 read 0x5A, the API's version and feature bits.

Stock bugs not repeated: IO0 stayed low after a download reset until the next normal one (it is also
the codec's clock); `GPIO_IN` always read 0; the backlight's register read 0 until written; a WS2812
trigger with the mode off hung with more than four LEDs.

## The firmware

Zephyr on one core, from the flash behind the badge's bootloader.

**UARTs** (`uart_dma.c`): Zephyr's PL011 driver sets up the line, this file owns DMACR and DR through the
hal's DMA calls: Zephyr's DMA driver has no ring mode, and its devicetree node stays off so that it
does not take channels. Receive runs for good into a ring (16 KiB for the ESP32, 4 KiB for the FPGA);
the channel's remaining count says how far it got. Transmit takes what lies together in a 4 KiB
ring, its end interrupt starts the next piece.

**USB** (`usb_vendor.c`, `usb_dev.c`): every function is one class on one bulk engine; a thread moves
the data (`bridge.c`), woken by completions and the UARTs' DMA and every millisecond for what the
UARTs bring. Two things learnt on the badge:

- Zephyr's CDC ACM class sends one packet per buffer and goes through a thread for each. That
  misses the frame nearly every time: 69 kB/s where the UART brings 200. The CDC ports here send
  buffers of 1 KiB, re-armed per packet in the interrupt.
- The controller ends an OUT transfer at a short packet or a full buffer, and Linux's cdc-acm and the
  WebUSB tools send no zero-length packet after a write of whole packets. With larger buffers the
  end of such a write waited for more to come, so their OUT buffers are one packet each (with
  a thread between packets: 150 to 190 kB/s, enough for esptool's 92). Interface 6's host ends
  every write with a short packet and gets 1 KiB buffers: 448 kB/s at 5 Mbaud.
- An IN endpoint with one buffer is empty when the host's next token comes before the interrupt has
  refilled it; the host gets a NAK and comes back much later: 520 to 660 kB/s. TinyUSB's way, two
  buffers and an interrupt per pair, leaves it empty after each pair: 553 kB/s. Two buffers and an
  interrupt for each (`CONFIG_UDC_RPI_PICO_DOUBLE_BUFFER_BULK_IN`, in our Zephyr) give 928 kB/s.

The thread moves what is there when it looks and then looks again, rather than until a ring is empty.
Bytes can come as fast as it takes them, in buffers of a few bytes: at 2 Mbaud it once stayed in that
loop for a whole 10 s transfer, and the watchdog below rebooted the badge.

A closed CDC port is not told apart from a slow one on USB: what nobody reads for 200 ms is dropped, so
the next reader gets no old news.

**I2C** (`regmap.c`, `i2c_regs.c`): bytes come and go in the driver's interrupt, the effects of writes wait
in a work item until the stop, in ascending register order, as in the stock firmware; CRESET moves
at the stop itself. CDONE is read when the ESP32 asks, which it does right after the bitstream's last
byte. The interrupt can be 100 us late, code running from flash (where that comes from was not found).
That is three bytes at 400 kHz, and the ESP32's load writes, reads and writes with little time between:
the DesignWare driver then took one transaction's pointer for the last one's data, in 5 to 15 % of the
boots, which left the FPGA unloaded. Our Zephyr tells transactions apart by the flag on a first byte.

**SELECT** (`qspi_select.c`) is the flash's chip select: it can only be read from RAM with every
interrupt off and the pin let go, some 30 us every 30 ms. DMA goes on meanwhile.

**Boot**: the bootloader starts the image with the clocks up and XIP running; the watchdog's scratch
registers are read and cleared first thing (the bootloader looks at them, and a crash leaves its
record there: register 18). The ESP32 stays in reset until the I2C target runs.

**Watchdog**: 2 s, fed by the bridge thread, the lowest of the firmware's. Anything that hangs reboots
the badge, the ESP32 with it, rather than need its power cut: a DMA abort that waited for ever did
exactly that on the desk before this was there. It is off for the ROM's bootloader.

## The framed link

`apps/osmosdr_serial`'s plain transport on the UART sends blocks of 4096 bytes whatever they hold, has
no checksum and resynchronises on a magic number. On the badge's UART, without flow control wires,
`esp_sdr/esdr_link.h` replaces it: a frame says what it is and how many came before it, and a lost byte
costs that frame, not the stream.

- **Frames**: type, flags, sequence number, payload, CRC-32, the whole COBS encoded and ended by a
  zero. Requests and replies carry `esdr_proto.h`'s frames as they are, so the handlers did not
  change; a block goes by its length.
- **Who speaks what**: the firmware listens for both at all times. A host that says hello gets frames;
  a plain host opens with thousands of zeros, and 64 empty frames in a row go back to the plain
  transport. Nothing needs to be told or configured, and a plain host still works on either RP2040
  firmware. (The plan had the ESP32 ask the RP2040's registers; this also works on any bridge, and
  follows the host rather than the firmware.)
- **Credit**: the host grants wire bytes (16 KiB at hello, more as it reads) and the ESP32 sends a block
  only with credit for it, so that a host that stops reading stops the sender, instead of the
  RP2040's 16 KiB ring overrunning. A new burst simply waits: its gap to the last is flagged, as ever.
- **Rate**: `ESDR_REQ_SET_LINK_BAUD`, in the old rate's reply; both ends move and the host says
  hello again. A host that finds no answer at 2 Mbaud (its predecessor went away at 5) resets the
  ESP32 over USB and starts anew; on closing it goes back to 2 Mbaud for whoever comes next.

The hosts: `FramedLink` in `apps/osmosdr/soapy/SoapyEsdr.cpp`, found by itself as
`driver=esdr,badge=<serial>` and giving its callers the padded blocks they expect;
`EsdrFramed` in `apps/osmosdr_serial/tools/esdr_serial.py` (port `usb`). Both over libusb on
interface 6.

On the UART the limit is the ESP32's time. A burst takes 2 to 3 ms to capture and decimate, in which
the UART's FIFO runs dry, and its 4 KiB take 8 ms at 5 Mbaud: some 80 bursts a second of the 520 the
library could make. So blocks also have a way that needs no CPU.

## The fast line

Blocks go from the ESP32 by SPI DMA into the FPGA, from there on a UART of its own to the RP2040, which
merges them onto interface 6 with what the ESP32's UART brings. The requests, replies and text stay on
that UART; the host grants credit and asks for the fast line with the same few words.

```
ESP32 --SPI3 DMA, 40 MHz--> FPGA 32 KiB FIFO --15 Mbaud--> RP2040 PIO --DMA--> 32 KiB ring --+
  |                                   ^                       |                              +--> USB
  +----- UART0 (requests, replies)    +--- ready (GPIO24) ----+  UART0 16 KiB ring ----------+
```

- **FPGA** (`gateware/esdr_fpga/link.py`, a bitstream of its own, `link.bin`, 599 of 5280 cells, 44.87
  MHz against the 30 asked for): SPI frames with opcode 0x20 (`ESDR_LINK_FPGA_OP`) go into a FIFO in
  one SPRAM block, and out at 30 MHz / 2 = 15 Mbaud, 8N1, on pin 9. A low ready line on pin 6 holds it
  back at the next frame's end. Bit 1 of register CAPS says the bitstream has all this; registers
  count what is free, sent, lost and held.
- **RP2040** (`COPROC_REQ_FPGA_FASTLINK`, `uart_dma.c`): the FPGA's two UART pins change hands while the
  line is on. GPIO25 goes to a PIO state machine at 8 times the bit rate and through DMA into a ring,
  GPIO24 is the ready line: low above 24 KiB in the ring, high again below 12 KiB. `bridge.c` merges the
  two rings at zero bytes, so that frames stay whole; a frame that does not go on (a boot message has
  no zero) gives way after 10 ms.
- **ESP32** (`apps/osmosdr_serial`, `CONFIG_APP_LINK_FAST`): the host's hello says it wants the line; the
  device says yes if the FPGA is loaded and has the capability, else it stays on the UART, and so does
  a host that does not ask. Blocks and other frames have sequence numbers of their own, for they may
  overtake each other. The credit (32 KiB) bounds what is under way, so the FIFO cannot overflow.

An FPGA with another bitstream on it answers no to the capability and leaves the pins to their UART.

## Measurements

On the badge, the ESP32 sending and checking a counter on its UART through the firmware to the host, 5 s
per case (`uart_overrun`, `ring_overrun` 0 throughout; nothing lost in any case):

| rate | ESP32 to host, CDC | host to ESP32, CDC | ESP32 to host, interface 6 | host to ESP32, interface 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Mbaud | 200 kB/s | 151 to 187 kB/s | 200 kB/s | 201 kB/s |
| 4 Mbaud | 400 kB/s | 152 to 188 kB/s | 400 kB/s | 400 kB/s |
| 5 Mbaud | 500 kB/s | 152 kB/s | 499 kB/s | 448 kB/s |

A longer run, 60 s each way at 2 and 5 Mbaud on both routes, 168 MB in all, lost nothing either.
5 Mbaud is the ESP32's limit. USB alone (`COPROC_TEST_SOURCE`, a counter from the firmware to a host
reading 64 KiB at a time) gives 928 kB/s, no word lost.

`apps/osmosdr_serial` by its average rate of samples (stream rates as the app's README has them): through
the FPGA 7.5 times the plain transport's at 2 MS/s and 9 times at 250 kS/s. 120 s through the FPGA at 2
MS/s, 84.9 MB: no bad or lost frame, no framing error, the ready line never low, the ring at most 3 KiB.
That is 707 kB/s of the 928 USB could take and the 1430 the line could: USB is no longer the limit, the
ESP32's cycle is, 5.9 ms a burst at 2 MS/s where SPI takes 0.8 ms. Where the rest goes was not measured.
One start of the plain
transport failed once with `SET_FREQ: Invalid argument`, right after a 250 kS/s run; 46 later starts
did not fail, 16 of them in that same order, 30 before and 30 after the idle sleep went from 1 ms (200
bytes at 2 Mbaud, against the ESP32's FIFO of 128) to 100 us, so that change is reasoned, not proven.

Checked by hand: every button with SELECT and the joystick as the ESP32 reads them (twice), the launcher
by joystick after a cold start, the backlight's steps, the IR LED in a phone's camera.
`integration.esdr_fpga` passes (4 of 4) through this firmware, the FPGA's bitstream loaded at 240 of 240
resets of the ESP32 under that test's image, 24 of 24 under MicroPython (12 resets, 12 reboots of the
RP2040). Not checked for want of hardware: WS2812 (no
LEDs on the badge's SAO port); the FPGA's UART loopback needs a bitstream that talks.
